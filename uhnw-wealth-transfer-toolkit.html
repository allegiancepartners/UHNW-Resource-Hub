Annual exclusion gifting

The annual gift tax exclusion is $19,000 per recipient for 2026 ($38,000 per recipient if a married couple elects gift-splitting) — gifts at or below this amount use no lifetime exemption and require no gift tax return. A disciplined annual gifting program, sustained over a multi-decade horizon and across multiple beneficiaries, can move substantial value out of the taxable estate before any lifetime exemption is touched.

NY estate tax context

New York imposes its own estate tax on top of the federal tax, with a distinctive "cliff": the state's basic exclusion amount is $7,350,000 for 2026, but estates above roughly 105% of that amount (about $7,717,500) see the exclusion phased out — the closer an estate sits to the cliff, the more of the exclusion is lost, producing a punitive effective marginal rate in that band before the top statutory rate of 16% applies further up. This makes keeping assets outside the taxable estate — via the Dynasty Trust and, at the appropriate stage, an estate freeze — especially valuable for New York residents. See the Estate Freeze tab.
Assets already funded into an irrevocable trust can keep compounding under an Estate Freeze — a well-established, easily explained structure that uses a Grantor or Non-Grantor Trust election, with no specialized insurance product required. If the family elects grantor trust status, they pay the trust's income tax personally each year — the trust grows tax-free, and the payment itself is treated as an additional tax-free gift to the trust. Combined with the assets sitting entirely outside the taxable estate, this can dramatically outperform holding everything personally.
